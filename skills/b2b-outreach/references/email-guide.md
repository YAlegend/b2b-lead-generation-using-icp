# Cold Email Guide — Phase 4

## Non-Negotiable Rules

- Email 1: 120 words MAX. No exceptions.
- Email 2: 80 words MAX.
- Email 3 (breakup): 60 words MAX.
- Subject line: under 8 words, no clickbait, no ALL CAPS
- One CTA per email — always a soft ask (20-min call)
- No attachments on Email 1
- No "I hope this email finds you well"
- No "I wanted to reach out because..."
- First line must reference something specific about THEM, not you

## Structure — Email 1

Line 1: Personalisation hook (their product, recent news, job post, or funding)
Lines 2–3: Their likely pain point — stated as an observation, not an assumption
Lines 4–5: What you do and one specific proof point (client name or outcome stat)
Line 6: Soft CTA (20-min call)

## Structure — Email 2 (Follow-Up Day 5)

Line 1: "Bumping this in case it got buried."
Lines 2–3: Add ONE piece of value (a relevant insight, stat, or short resource)
Line 4: Repeat soft CTA

## Structure — Email 3 (Breakup Day 12)

Line 1: Acknowledge they are probably busy or not interested right now
Line 2: Leave the door open ("if the timing changes...")
Line 3: Warm sign-off — no guilt, no pressure

## Tone by Industry

Adapt the email tone to match the company's actual industry and buyer:

- **Professional services (legal, finance, consulting):** Formal, precise, evidence-led.
  Buyers are risk-averse — lead with outcomes and credibility, not personality.
- **Technology / SaaS:** Concise, peer-level. Reference integration, scale, or speed.
  Avoid buzzwords. CTOs and engineers respect brevity and specificity.
- **Creative / agency:** Warmer, more conversational. Show taste and point of view.
  Buyers are buying judgment as much as output.
- **Healthcare / regulated industries:** Measured, credentialed. Reference compliance
  or regulatory outcomes where relevant. Never overstate claims.
- **Industrial / manufacturing:** Conservative, reliability-focused. Buyers want
  proof before conversation. Reference a named client or measurable result.
- **Recruitment / staffing:** Direct and timeline-focused. Buyers feel urgency.
  Lead with speed and quality of placement, not process.
- **Consumer / retail:** Outcome-oriented with a commercial lens. Reference revenue,
  conversion, or customer retention — not creative quality alone.

These are guides, not rules. Read the company's own voice and match it.

## Subject Line Formulas That Work

- "[Their company name] + [specific outcome]"
- "Quick question about [their product or initiative]"
- "How [similar company] [achieved outcome]"
- "Re: [topic they posted about on LinkedIn]" (only if true)
- "[Mutual connection] suggested I reach out" (only if true)

## What to Avoid

- Mentioning your company name in the subject line
- Starting with "My name is..." or "I work at..."
- Listing more than one pain point — pick the sharpest one
- Asking for a meeting AND information AND a reply in the same email
- Anything that reads like it was sent to 1,000 people
